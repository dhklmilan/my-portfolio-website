Analysis Summary

On 26 August 2026, the disaster began with a glacier related slope failure that generated an ice and catastrophic debris flow, and the flood was likely triggered from the border with China. The slope failure generated energy equivalent to a M5.2 earthquake (USGS). This debris flow and flooding travelled nearly 100 km and impacted populated areas downstream along the Rasuwagadhi border post between China and Nepal. Based on this preliminary imagery analysis, more than 9,000 buildings and infrastructure have likely been affected and destroyed, including vital highways: Prithvi Highway, Pasang Lhamu Highway, Trishuli–Mailung–Syabrubesi Road, and 11 hydropower projects. The attached photo below presents temperature and hot period dynamics of the flood origin region near the China border over the 2016 to 2026 period. The maximum temperature has not consistently increased; however, the hot periods have generally become more persistent in recent years, specifically after 2020. The exceptionally prolonged 24-day warm spell from 13 July to 5 August 2024 represents the longest hot period in the record, while 2025 and 2026 also show several prolonged warm periods. Note: This is a preliminary analysis using Sentinel-2 imagery (10 m) and the water index NDWI, which has not been validated in the field
